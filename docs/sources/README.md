## Numbering is edition-specific

Agamodaya counts 13 sutras in the first lesson. Jacobi 1882 has 7 sections. The app uses Jacobi 1884 numbering (7 units). Concordance, from the extraction in [acharanga-1-1.sources.json](acharanga-1-1.sources.json):

| App unit | Agamodaya sutra | Jacobi 1882 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1.1.1 | 1 (start of 2) | §1 |
| 1.1.2 | 1 to 2 (the "some do not know" clause and the direction list) | §2 |
| 1.1.3 | 3 | §3 |
| 1.1.4 | 4 | §4 |
| 1.1.5 | 5 to 7 | §5 |
| 1.1.6 | 8 to 9 | §6 |
| 1.1.7 | 10 to 13 | §7 |

The pairing of 1.1.1 and 1.1.2 is the least certain. A sutra ID is valid only inside its edition.

## Method (per unit)

1. Take the Prakrit from Agamodaya; compare with Jacobi 1882. Record every difference and OCR doubt.
2. Take meaning from the Sanskrit equivalent and Śīlāṅka's commentary; compare with Jacobi 1884 and, as a check only, the 1999 Hindi.
3. Write the Hindi in our own words, sutra by sutra, in source order. Do not copy any source's sentences.
4. Mark status: `draft` (written), `compared` (agrees with at least two independent sources), `reviewed` (a qualified person looked), `approved` (owner accepted). Nothing is `reviewed` yet.
5. Ask a scholar only about flagged differences, not the whole text.

## Known OCR doubts (first lesson)

पुरथिमाओ (read पुरत्थिमाओ), पञ्चत्थिमाओ, उड्डाओ, अस्थि (अत्थि), हेडं (हेउं), लोगसि (लोगंसि), परिणाया (परिण्णाया), line break in अ-दिसाओ. Sutra 6 differs between editions (कारवेसुं, a past form, vs Jacobi's future form). All need a check against the scan pages given in the JSON.
